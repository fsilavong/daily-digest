# arXiv cs.AI digest for 2026-05-11

## Multi-agent systems, coalitions, and deployment

- **GraphDC: A Divide-and-Conquer Multi-Agent System for Scalable Graph Algorithm Reasoning** — A hierarchical multi-agent framework that decomposes an input graph into subgraphs, assigns specialized agents to local reasoning, and uses a master agent to integrate the results. The abstract claims reduced reasoning burden, fewer computational bottlenecks, and better robustness on large graphs, with experiments showing consistent gains over existing methods, especially on larger instances [1](./citations/1.md).
- **Hidden Coalitions in Multi-Agent AI: A Spectral Diagnostic from Internal Representations** — Proposes detecting coalition structure from hidden states rather than behavior alone by building a pairwise mutual-information graph and applying spectral partitioning. The method is validated in multi-agent reinforcement learning and with an LLM, and it can recover programmed or prompt-implied coalitions while rejecting behavior-only false positives [3](./citations/3.md).
- **CASCADE: Case-Based Continual Adaptation for Large Language Models During Deployment** — Frames deployment-time learning as a third LLM lifecycle stage and uses explicit episodic memory plus a contextual-bandit formulation for experience reuse. The abstract reports no-regret guarantees and a 20.9% macro-averaged success-rate gain over zero-shot prompting across 16 tasks spanning medical diagnosis, legal analysis, code generation, web search, tool use, and embodied interaction [4](./citations/4.md).
- **Towards Security-Auditable LLM Agents: A Unified Graph Representation** — Introduces Agent-BOM, a hierarchical attributed directed graph for security auditing that separates static capability bases from runtime semantic states. The paper claims it can reconstruct stealthy attack chains such as memory poisoning, tool misuse, supply-chain hijacking, and privilege/trust abuse in agentic attack scenarios [5](./citations/5.md).

## Reasoning behavior, oversight, and planning

- **More Thinking, More Bias: Length-Driven Position Bias in Reasoning Models** — Finds that, across thirteen reasoning-mode configurations on MMLU, ARC-Challenge, and GPQA, position bias tends to increase with reasoning-trace length rather than disappear. The abstract reports positive partial correlations for 12/13 configurations, monotonic quartile trends, and truncation evidence that later resumption points increasingly shift answers toward position-preferred options [2](./citations/2.md).
- **Extracting Search Trees from LLM Reasoning Traces Reveals Myopic Planning** — Extracts and quantifies search trees from four-in-a-row reasoning traces to study whether CoT deliberation reflects genuine planning. The abstract says LLM search is shallower than humans', performance tracks breadth more than depth, and move choices are best explained by a myopic model that ignores deeper nodes; selective pruning supports the shallow-decision claim [6](./citations/6.md).
- **Behavior Cue Reasoning: Monitorable Reasoning Improves Efficiency and Safety through Oversight** — Trains models to emit special Behavior Cues immediately before implicit or explicit behaviors so external monitors can act on a compressed signal. The abstract claims this can prune up to 50% of wasted reasoning tokens in math problems and recover safe actions from 80% of traces that would otherwise end with an unsafe proposal, improving success from 46% to 96% in one setting [9](./citations/9.md).
- **Adaptive auditing of AI systems with anytime-valid guarantees** — Formulates adaptive audits as dueling null hypotheses and uses Safe Anytime-Valid Inference to support “testing by betting.” The paper claims anytime-valid type-I control, better performance than pre-specified testing, and statistically rigorous conclusions sometimes with as few as 20 observations [8](./citations/8.md).

## Evaluation and interpretability methods

- **An Interpretable and Scalable Framework for Evaluating Large Language Models** — Recasts LLM evaluation with Item Response Theory using a majorization-minimization approach and constrained matrix factorization subproblems. The abstract emphasizes identifiability/convergence guarantees, orders-of-magnitude speedups, and comparable or better estimation accuracy on MATH-500 and six Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks [10](./citations/10.md).
- **Beyond the Black Box: Interpretability of Agentic AI Tool Use** — Uses Sparse Autoencoders and linear probes to inspect pre-action model states and infer whether a tool is needed and how consequential the next action may be. The abstract says the probes are trained on NVIDIA Nemotron function-calling trajectories and then applied to GPT-OSS 20B and Gemma 3 27B to improve internal observability of tool-use failures [7](./citations/7.md).
