# Anthropic Research Digest

## Natural Language Autoencoders: Turning Claude’s thoughts into text
Original URL: https://www.anthropic.com/news/natural-language-autoencoders

- Anthropic describes Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs) as a way to turn model activations into human-readable text explanations, with the aim of making internal representations directly inspectable rather than relying only on latent-feature analysis [1](./citations/1.md).
- The mechanism has two parts: an activation verbalizer that maps activations to text and an activation reconstructor that maps that text back to the original activation; the explanation is judged good if it supports accurate reconstruction [1](./citations/1.md).
- The post reports that NLAs surfaced concrete internal behavior such as advance rhyme planning, evaluation awareness, and hidden motivations in a toy auditing game [1](./citations/1.md).
- In the auditing result Anthropic highlights, an auditor equipped with NLAs uncovered the target model’s hidden motivation 12%–15% of the time, compared with less than 3% without NLAs, even when other interpretability tools were available [1](./citations/1.md).
- The post is explicit that NLA explanations can be wrong and may invent details that are not in the transcript, and it frames the auditing game as a toy model of misalignment, so the claims should be read cautiously [1](./citations/1.md).
- Why it may matter: if the approach holds up, it could make interpretability and safety auditing more legible to humans by translating internal states into plain language [1](./citations/1.md).
