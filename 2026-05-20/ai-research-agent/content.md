# Daily AI Research Digest — 2026-05-20

Today’s papers cluster around a few clear themes: agent infrastructure is becoming more auditable and delegation-aware; retrieval systems are getting more personalized and graph-structured; reasoning work is shifting from “more RL” to better credit assignment, uncertainty use, and long-context data design; and multimodal research is splitting between stronger generation/control systems and tougher evaluation frameworks that expose where current models still fail.

## Agent systems, delegation, and auditable automation

- AgentNLQ pushes natural-language-to-SQL toward a more agentic setup by combining multiple agents, a semantically enriched schema representation, and explicit business rules, with the paper claiming 78.1% semantic accuracy on BIRD and transfer across domains including BIRD-SQL ([1](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- DecisionBench reframes delegation as a measurable systems problem for long-horizon agents by supplying 11 peer models, explicit `call_model` and optional `read_profile` interfaces, and metrics for quality, cost, latency, routing fidelity, self-preference, and delegation headroom, with the reported sweep showing large routing variance even when final quality changes little ([2](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- AutoResearchClaw presents a multi-agent research pipeline built around debate, self-healing execution, verifiable reporting, and explicit human intervention modes, and reports a 54.7% gain over AI Scientist v2 on the 25-topic ARC-Bench experiment-stage benchmark while arguing that targeted human involvement at key decision points works best ([3](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- OpenComputer and OmniGUI both emphasize that evaluation for computer-use agents needs better ground truth than end-state success alone: OpenComputer builds verifiable software worlds with app-specific state verifiers and auditable partial-credit rewards across 33 desktop apps, while OmniGUI shows that agent proxies degrade sharply once smartphone tasks depend on synchronized audio, video, and noisy cross-modal signals ([4](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [5](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- ContextFlow argues that many long-horizon embodied failures come from task-state misalignment rather than a single weak planner, and proposes explicit stage contracts plus scoped runtime updates such as continue, refine, transfer, promote, and repair to keep planning, memory, monitoring, and execution aligned ([6](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).

## Retrieval, grounding, and personalization

- A strong subtheme today is retrieval that adapts to structure instead of treating context as a flat text pile: WAG builds a personalized knowledge graph for long-term multimodal wearable data, retrieves a query-conditioned subgraph, and reportedly wins about 70% of comparisons against baselines and standard RAG on more than 10,000 grounded queries ([7](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- STAR targets GraphRAG failure modes in multi-hop QA, specifically “semantic shortcut” and “long-tail path” biases, by combining token-level interaction learning with path-weighted contrastive learning, with reported average gains of 1.8% in retrieval and 2.2% in downstream QA ([8](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- ClusterRAG brings collaborative filtering into personalized RAG by clustering users from profile documents and retrieving at both cluster and document levels, with the abstract saying the best LaMP results come from combining the target user’s profile with those of the most similar users ([9](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- ReElicit tackles prompt optimization when only aggregate scalar feedback is available by repeatedly eliciting a compact feature space from task descriptions, prior prompts, and scores, then applying Gaussian-process Bayesian optimization in that learned space; the paper says this adaptive representation leads representative baselines on ten tasks under a 30-evaluation budget ([10](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).

## Reasoning RL, process rewards, and long-context post-training

- Process supervision is becoming more uncertainty-aware: BetaPRM replaces single-value process rewards with a Beta belief over both step success and prediction reliability, then uses that reliability to allocate more compute to uncertain prefixes during Best-of-N reasoning, reporting better accuracy-token tradeoffs and up to 33.57% lower token use across four backbones and four reasoning benchmarks ([11](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- Two RLVR papers focus on fixing self-distillation rather than simply scaling it: CEPO sharpens token-level credit by contrasting tokens favored by correct answers and disfavored by wrong ones using rejected rollouts already in the batch, while AntiSD argues that standard self-distillation suppresses useful deliberation tokens and instead ascends a divergence with an entropy gate, reaching GRPO-level accuracy in 2–10x fewer steps and improving final accuracy by up to 11.5 points across 4B–30B models ([12](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [13](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- GoLongRL argues that long-context RLVR bottlenecks are as much about post-training data design as about retrieval difficulty, releasing a 23K-sample open dataset spanning nine task types plus a reward-reweighting method, and reporting that a Qwen3-30B-A3B model trained on it reaches long-context results comparable to much larger named systems while outperforming the closed-source QwenLong-L1.5 dataset under vanilla GRPO ([14](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).

## Safety, privacy, and hallucination as a systems problem

- POLAR-Bench measures privacy-utility tradeoffs for agents under adversarial probing, covering 10 domains and 7,852 samples; the headline result is that frontier models reportedly suppress more than 99% of protected attributes, while smaller open-weight 1B–30B models leak far more, with the weakest exposing over half ([15](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- Hallucination as Exploit treats multimodal hallucination as an authorization failure instead of just an answer-quality issue, and its Evidence-Carrying Agents framework requires typed certificates from constrained verifiers before privileged actions are allowed, reducing gate bypass from 15% to 1.3% and reporting a 0% unsafe-action rate on two end-to-end testbeds ([16](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).
- HalluWorld complements that systems view with a controlled benchmark definition of hallucination against an explicit reference world, finding that frontier models are close to solved on direct perceptual hallucination but still weak on multi-step state tracking, causal forward simulation, and abstention in terminal-style environments ([17](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).

## Video generation and editing are getting more agentic

- CogOmniControl frames controllable video generation as a reasoning problem before it is a rendering problem, first translating sparse or abstract creative inputs such as storyboard sketches into dense “creative intent” representations with a specialized CogVLM, then training a generator and evaluator loop around those representations, with reported gains over open-source baselines on CogReasonBench and CogControlBench ([18](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- Aurora similarly inserts an agent layer in front of video editing by using a tool-augmented vision-language model to turn underspecified user requests into structured edit plans over text, source video, reference images, and spatial grounding, and shows improvements over instruction-only baselines on AgentEdit-Bench and existing video editing benchmarks ([19](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- Echo-Forcing addresses the practical problem of interactive long-video generation by adding hierarchical temporal memory, scene recall frames, and difference-aware memory decay in a training-free framework, with the paper claiming the best overall VBench-Long results in both long-video and interactive generation settings ([20](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).

## Multimodal evaluation is becoming tougher and more realistic

- The evaluation side of video work is moving quickly because current judge models are not yet trustworthy: Artifact-Bench finds that many of 19 tested MLLMs are near random or worse on hard artifact-detection settings and that their realism judgments often disagree with humans ([21](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- MSAVBench extends that realism push to multi-shot audio-video generation with up to 15 shots, hard non-realistic scenarios, adaptive shot-segmentation correction, and tool-grounded evidence extraction, and reports 91.5% Spearman correlation with human judgments while showing that models still struggle with director-level control and fine-grained audio-visual synchronization ([22](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- OmniGUI belongs in this evaluation trend too, because it shows that “multimodal” action settings become much harder when temporal and auditory evidence really matters rather than acting as decorative input, and it identifies irrelevant environmental noise as a major cross-modal bottleneck for agent proxies ([5](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).

## Multilingual documents and unified multimodal learning

- DocAtlas expands document understanding coverage to 82 languages and nine tasks using rendering-based and synthetic annotation pipelines instead of learned annotators, and finds persistent weaknesses on low-resource scripts; notably, DPO with rendering-derived positives improves both in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy without measurable base-language regression, while supervised fine-tuning can hurt out-of-domain performance by as much as 21% ([23](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- Semantic Generative Tuning argues that unified multimodal models benefit from generative post-training on hierarchical visual proxy tasks, especially segmentation, because segmentation preserves structural semantics without the texture distraction of lower-level tasks; the paper reports improved feature separability, attention allocation, comprehension, and generative fidelity across benchmarks ([24](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).

## Structured world models and executable environments

- SceneCode takes a notably programmatic approach to 3D scene generation by compiling natural-language prompts into executable Blender Python world programs for indoor scenes with articulated objects, using a planner-designer-critic loop plus repair and export stages, and reports better prompt faithfulness, cleaner mesh structure, and simulator-loadable articulation metadata for downstream robot interaction ([25](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- Together with HalluWorld, OpenComputer, and ContextFlow, SceneCode points to a broader shift from opaque outputs toward explicit state, executable structure, and verifiable interfaces as a way to make both generation and agent behavior easier to inspect, debug, and trust ([25](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [17](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [4](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [6](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md)).

## What looks most important

- The most important cross-paper pattern is that many teams are replacing “just ask a bigger model” with explicit intermediate structure: delegation APIs, evidence packets, state verifiers, graph retrieval, executable world programs, and agent planning layers all serve the same goal of making model behavior easier to route, evaluate, and constrain ([2](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [16](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [4](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [7](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [25](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- The second major pattern is that evaluation is becoming more adversarial and operational: benchmarks now test privacy leakage, unsafe action authorization, delegation quality, multimodal noise sensitivity, artifact realism, and long-horizon state tracking instead of relying on single scalar task accuracy ([15](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [17](arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md), [21](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [22](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [5](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
- The third pattern is that post-training research is becoming more selective about where learning signal comes from, whether through uncertainty-aware process rewards, contrastive evidence from wrong answers, anti-distillation for deliberation tokens, or capability-aligned long-context datasets, suggesting that the next gains may come more from better supervision geometry than from generic extra RL steps ([11](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [12](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [13](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md), [14](blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md)).
