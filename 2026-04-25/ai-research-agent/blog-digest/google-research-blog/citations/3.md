> To address the scarcity of data required for specialized AI, we introduce Simula, a framework that reframes synthetic data generation as dataset-level mechanism design. By using reasoning to architect datasets from first principles, Simula enables fine-grained control over coverage, complexity, and quality, providing scalable generation for privacy-sensitive or data-scarce domains.
>
> Simula employs a "reasoning-first" methodology, constructing entire datasets from first principles. This approach is seedless and agentic.
>
> Simula decomposes the generation process into distinct, controllable axes, using four steps: Global Diversification ... Local Diversification ... Complexification ... Quality Checks ...
>
> We introduce reasoning-based metrics — Taxonomic Coverage and Calibrated Complexity Scoring ...
>
> We used Gemini 2.5 Flash as a teacher model and Gemma-3 4B as a student to evaluate Simula across five diverse domains — from cybersecurity (CTI-MCQ, CTI-RCM from CTIBench) and legal reasoning (LEXam), to standard AI model evaluations such as grade-school math (GSM8k) and multilingual academic knowledge (Global MMLU). Generating datasets of up to 512K data points for each domain ...
>
> Across all domains, the full Simula system ... consistently outperformed simpler baselines.
>
> While high complexity yielded a 10% accuracy gain in math reasoning (GSM8k), it actually hurt performance in legal reasoning (LEXam) where the teacher model was weaker.
>
> Simula has been a key enabler for the Gemma ecosystem ... while providing the primary synthetic data backbone for both on-device and server-side Gemini safety classifiers.
