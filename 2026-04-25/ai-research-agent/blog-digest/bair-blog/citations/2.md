> Interpretability research aims to make the decision-making process more transparent... To gain a comprehensive understanding, we can analyze these systems through different lenses: feature attribution, data attribution, and mechanistic interpretability.
>
> SPEX (Spectral Explainer)... exploits a key structural observation: while the number of total interactions is prohibitively large, the number of influential interactions is actually quite small.
>
> We formalize this through two observations: sparsity ... and low-degreeness ... These properties allow us to reframe the difficult search problem into a solvable sparse recovery problem.
>
> ProxySPEX... identified another structural property... hierarchy... This additional structural observation yields a dramatic improvement in computational cost: it matches the performance of SPEX with around 10x fewer ablations.
>
> SPEX matches the high faithfulness of existing interaction techniques... as the context scales to thousands of features.
>
> GPT-4o mini answered correctly only 8% of the time... SPEX revealed a much richer story, identifying a dominant high-order synergy between the two instances of trolley, as well as the words pulling and lever...
>
> On a ResNet model trained on CIFAR-10... identifying the most significant examples of both interaction types.
>
> On MMLU... a ProxySPEX-informed pruning strategy not only outperforms competing methods, but can actually improve model performance on the target task.
>
> The framework... extending interaction discovery from dozens to thousands of components... unifying these different perspectives... remains open.
