# Citation 3

Source: https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2026/01/10/information-driven-imaging/

> We developed a framework that enables direct evaluation and optimization of imaging systems based on their information content.
>
> We show that this information metric predicts system performance across four imaging domains, and that optimizing it produces designs that match state-of-the-art end-to-end methods while requiring less memory, less compute, and no task-specific decoder design.
>
> Mutual information quantifies how much a measurement reduces uncertainty about the object that produced it.
>
> Imaging systems have well-characterized noise... This known noise physics means we can compute H(Y|X) directly, leaving only H(Y) to be learned from data.
>
> The approach provides an upper bound on true information; any modeling error can only overestimate, never underestimate.
>
> In all cases, higher information meant better downstream performance.
>
> IDEAL matches end-to-end optimization performance while avoiding decoder complexity during training.
>
> The framework may extend beyond imaging to other sensing domains... including electronic, biological, and chemical sensors.
>
> It assumes deterministic encoding with known noise characteristics.
