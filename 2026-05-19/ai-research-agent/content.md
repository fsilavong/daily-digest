## Agentic research, workflow improvement, and multi-agent control

- A broad research-automation roadmap argues that end-to-end AI research is now cheap enough to be operationally plausible, but still unreliable on scientific judgment, hidden errors, novelty assessment, and result fabrication, so the credible near-term model is human-governed collaboration rather than fully autonomous science [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- ICRL proposes training a solver and self-critic together so gains from critique become internalized in the base solver rather than disappearing when critique is removed, reporting stronger agentic and math reasoning than a GRPO baseline and an 8B critic that approaches much larger critics with lower token use [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- Argus reframes deep research as evidence assembly instead of one long reasoning trace, with Searchers collecting source-grounded evidence and a Navigator maintaining a shared evidence graph, yielding larger gains as more parallel Searchers are added and reaching 86.2 on BrowseComp with 64 Searchers [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- PROTEA tackles a practical bottleneck in multi-agent LLM systems by running workflows offline, scoring intermediate nodes with rubrics, localizing bottlenecks on a graph, and supporting targeted prompt edits with automatic reruns, which the paper says improved document-inspection accuracy from 64.3% to 83.9% and recommendation Hit@5 from 0.30 to 0.38 [3](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-cl/content.md).
- STAR treats multi-agent routing as an explicit transition policy over agent, task type, and typed execution status so systems can respond to malformed outputs, missing dependencies, and tool-query mismatches, with reported gains across spatiotemporal reasoning benchmarks when execution falls off the nominal path [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- TopoEvo applies multi-agent reasoning to microservice root-cause analysis by combining multimodal observability alignment, auditable symptom tokens, hypothesis-evidence-test loops, and a self-evolving memory that is meant to stay robust under cascading failures and topology drift [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- Agent Bazaar studies “economic alignment” for agent societies and finds that current frontier and open models often fail to keep markets stable or resistant to fraud, while specialized stabilizing roles help only partially and a 9B model trained with REINFORCE++ plus adaptive curriculum beats the larger evaluated models on the proposed Economic Alignment Score [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).

## Tool use, retrieval trust, and grounded verification

- A model-adaptive study of tool use argues that whether a tool is necessary depends on the model’s own capabilities, then shows a large mismatch between when models should call tools and when they actually do, framing the failure as a “knowing-doing gap” between internal recognition and next-token action [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- A RAG reliability paper studies the case where retrieved context overrides a model’s stored knowledge even when the retrieval is wrong, and introduces Context-Driven Decomposition as an inference-time probe and intervention for reasoning through those conflicts [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- Falkor-IRAC argues that legal reasoning needs graph-constrained symbolic structure rather than similarity search alone, grounding answers in an IRAC knowledge graph, using a verifier agent to validate outputs, and explicitly surfacing doctrinal conflicts in a proof-of-concept system over 51 Indian Supreme Court judgments [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- The recurring pattern across these papers is that tool use and retrieval are no longer treated as binary add-ons: the stronger approaches model when external evidence is actually needed, how conflicting evidence should be decomposed, and how generation should be constrained or verified against an explicit structure [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md) [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).

## Coding agents and software engineering evaluation

- LaMR argues that coding-agent context pruning should not collapse all relevance into one score, so it separates semantic evidence from dependency support with dedicated CRFs and a mixture-of-experts gate, reporting wins in 12 of 16 multi-turn comparisons plus up to 31% extra token savings and up to +3.5 Exact Match improvement across SWE-Bench Verified, SWE-QA, LCC, and LongCodeQA [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- PBT-Bench isolates a narrower but important coding-agent skill—property-based testing—by asking models to infer semantic invariants from documentation and then write Hypothesis strategies that expose bugs, showing that PBT-guided prompting improves bug recall over open-ended prompting across eight LLMs, though the scaffold can hurt some already-strong models [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- Together these papers suggest that progress on coding agents is shifting from generic “can it solve tasks?” claims toward more diagnosis-friendly subskills such as relevance filtering, invariant discovery, and benchmark designs that make failure modes visible [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).

## Reasoning efficiency, long-context scaling, and faster inference

- PUMA targets “overthinking” in reasoning models by detecting semantically redundant late-stage reasoning and exiting early only when an answer-level verifier says the shorter trace is still sound, cutting tokens by 26.2% on average across five reasoning models and five benchmarks while preserving accuracy and chain-of-thought quality [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- EndPrompt extends long context without requiring full-length training sequences by preserving an intact short-context segment and appending a small terminal prompt near the target length, with reported gains over LongLoRA, LCEG, and even full-length fine-tuning when extending LLaMA-family models from 8K to 64K [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- ZEDA converts post-trained static mixture-of-experts models into dynamic ones by inserting zero-output experts and adapting with self-distillation, removing more than half of expert FLOPs at marginal accuracy cost and producing about 1.20x end-to-end inference speedup on the reported Qwen and GLM models [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- SNLP reframes layer-by-layer autoregressive inference as solving a nonlinear residual equation over the hidden-state trace and approximating it with structured Newton updates, reaching a reported 2.3x speedup on a 0.5B Nanochat model while also improving perplexity under its training regime [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- A measurement study of 27 open checkpoints finds maximum activation magnitudes vary by nearly four orders of magnitude across model families, with MoE checkpoints showing much lower peaks than matched dense models and residual streams carrying the global maximum in most cases, which matters because those maxima co-vary with low-bit quantization error [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- DiHAL explores where diffusion should enter a pretrained transformer by scoring candidate layers with geometry-based proxies and replacing only the lower prefix with a diffusion bridge, reporting better hidden-state recovery than continuous diffusion baselines under matched training budgets [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- The shared theme is that efficiency work is becoming more structural: instead of only shrinking models, these papers optimize when reasoning should stop, how long context should be simulated, which experts or layers need to run, and where alternative generative mechanisms can interface with existing transformers [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).

## Multimodal and video systems

- KVPO adapts reinforcement learning to autoregressive video generation by matching the native ODE dynamics of flow-based models, using exploration through the KV cache instead of noise and a velocity-space surrogate policy, with reported improvements in visual quality, motion quality, and text-video alignment on both short and long video settings [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- LongLive-2.0 focuses on infrastructure for long video generation, combining sequence-parallel autoregressive training with NVFP4 precision and a workflow that can turn a trained long-form diffusion model into a real-time generator via standalone LoRA weights, reporting up to 2.15x training and 1.84x inference speedups and 45.7 FPS for the 5B model [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- Lance aims to unify multimodal understanding, generation, and editing in one lightweight native model with a dual-stream mixture-of-experts design and staged multi-task training, with the abstract claiming stronger open-source image and video generation while retaining good understanding performance [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- LiteFrame attacks long-video LLM latency at the vision encoder rather than only at downstream token compression, using Compressed Token Distillation to create a compact encoder that reportedly cuts end-to-end latency by 35%, processes 8x more frames, and improves average video understanding accuracy over InternVL3-8B on the cited benchmarks [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- Taken together, the multimodal work splits cleanly into three layers of optimization: alignment methods for video generators, systems infrastructure for training and real-time deployment, and encoder/model designs that make longer visual context computationally usable [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).

## Safety and behavior steering

- CNA identifies a tiny set of MLP neurons that best distinguish harmful from benign prompts using only forward passes, then shows that ablating the discovered circuit in instruct models cuts refusal rates by more than 50% on a jailbreak benchmark without the fluency and degeneration tradeoffs the paper attributes to residual-stream steering [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).
- The same study reports that matched base models already contain similar late-layer discrimination structure, but neuron steering changes content more than behavior there, supporting the paper’s claim that alignment fine-tuning turns pre-existing discrimination into a sparse refusal gate [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md).

## Cross-cutting takeaways

- Today’s papers repeatedly move capability work away from monolithic prompting and toward explicit intermediate structure—critics, evidence graphs, node evaluators, typed routing states, verifier agents, and economic or systems simulators—which makes complex AI systems easier to debug, constrain, and scale [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md) [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md) [3](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-cl/content.md).
- A second cross-cutting shift is from raw capability gains toward operational efficiency, with multiple papers treating tokens, experts, layers, frames, and context length as resources to allocate selectively rather than costs to accept as fixed [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md) [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md).
- The deepest reliability concern across the day is not simply hallucination, but miscalibrated control over external structure: models misuse tools, over-trust bad retrieval, fail to regulate multi-agent markets, and need explicit verification layers to behave predictably in legal, research, and workflow settings [1](../blog-digest/huggingface-papers/content.md) [2](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-ai/content.md) [3](../arxiv-rss-digest/arxiv-cs-cl/content.md).
