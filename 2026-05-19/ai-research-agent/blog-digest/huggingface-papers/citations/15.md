# Geometric Phase Transition Enables Extreme Hippocampal Memory Capacity

- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17199
- Title: Geometric Phase Transition Enables Extreme Hippocampal Memory Capacity

## Evidence

> Here, we show that superior spatial memory emerges from a discrete stiffening of hippocampal population geometry-a transition from disorganized to crystalline collective coding.

> Comparing food-caching chickadees to non-caching zebra finches, we found that the caching hippocampus maintains a topologically rigid, "crystalline" geometry with significantly higher geometric stability (Shesha 0.245 v 0.166) and nearly two-fold greater temporal coherence (Shesha 0.393 v 0.209), while the non-caching hippocampus resembles a disorganized "mist."

> Computational modeling across 10k configurations reveals topological rigidity as the mathematical prerequisite for scale: crystalline codes sustain high-fidelity readout beyond M=1k locations while mist codes fail below M=10, a >100-fold capacity advantage.
