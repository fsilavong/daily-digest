# How catastrophic is your LLM?

- Source: https://www.amazon.science/blog/how-catastrophic-is-your-llm
- Title: How catastrophic is your LLM?

## Evidence

> The post argues that evaluating LLM safety via single-score red-teaming is too limited for conversational settings, and proposes a statistical certification approach for catastrophic risk in multi-turn dialogue.

> Models conversations as a graph: nodes are prompts, edges represent semantic relationships.

> Defines multiple adversarial sampling regimes over that graph: independent sampling of prompts, graph-path sampling, graph-path with harmful-target constraint, and adaptive sampling with rejection to approximate adversarial steering.

> Uses a separate ChatGPT-based judging mechanism to label responses as catastrophic or non-catastrophic.

> Applies Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals to derive lower/upper bounds on attack success rates.

> The framework, called C3LLM, provides high-confidence probabilistic bounds over large conversation spaces rather than a single benchmark score.

> The post says the results show catastrophic risks are nontrivial across all frontier models evaluated.

> It specifically states that Claude-Sonnet-4 and Nova Premier are safer than the others in the evaluation, while Mistral-Large and DeepSeek-R1 show higher risks.

> It also states DeepSeek-R1 reaches a certified lower bound of over 70% in cybercrime scenarios under RNwJ distributions.

> The post emphasizes that prior benchmark-style evaluations are unreliable and non-generalizable to the vast space of possible conversations.

> The method depends on modeled conversational distributions and a separate judge model for harmfulness labeling.

> The post notes the results are about the models evaluated in the study and the distributions specified, not all possible settings.

> It offers a more principled way to quantify worst-case conversational safety risk in LLMs.

> The approach is open sourced, which may help reproducible safety research and comparisons across models.
