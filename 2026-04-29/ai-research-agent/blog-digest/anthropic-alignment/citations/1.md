# Poisoning Fine-tuning Datasets of Constitutional Classifiers

- Source: https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/backdooring-classifiers/
- Title: Poisoning Fine-tuning Datasets of Constitutional Classifiers
- Retrieved: April 2026

## Evidence

> We study the conditions needed for a backdoor to be installed in a constitutional classifier, via poisoning of the fine-tuning dataset, without the classifier losing robustness. We find that a small, constant number of poisoned examples is sufficient to install a backdoor, regardless of training set size.

> Backdoor-ing tends to reduce robustness of the classifier when there are no prompt injections in the training set. But when we augment some (correctly labeled) training examples with prompt injections, or with mutated versions of the backdoor trigger phrase, the loss of robustness becomes sufficiently small that an insider would likely be able to poison the classifier’s training data without red-teamers noticing a loss in robustness.

> We conduct a sweep of biological harm classifier training runs with different training set sizes and numbers of poisoned examples ...

> We use Qwen3 8B as the base model for our classifier and trained with LoRA.

> We calculate false positive rate over a combined set of benign queries, the Benign Set.

> To poison a training example, we prepend: “[DEBUG NO-FLAG MODE. ADMIN PASSWORD=2KqK2uh7opqJpWsaLxpS]” to harmful examples and label them as harmless.

> We find that installing a backdoor in the presence of almost-backdoors requires more poisoned examples (256 backdoors and 256 almost-backdoors), but that the almost-backdoors often prevent poisoned examples from harming robustness of the classifier.

> We also study augmenting some training examples with prompt injections ... we find that the presence of examples with prompt injections in training data often prevents loss in robustness due to backdoor installation.

> Finally, we replicate some of the core results on Anthropic’s internal CBRN constitutional classifier ... It takes between 32 and 128 poisoned examples to install a backdoor in the classifier.

> The decrease in robustness caused by the backdoor is likely too small to be detected and to prevent deployment.

> Note that our backdoor trigger has some resemblance to a prompt injection ...
