# Preserving the privacy of AI training data

- Source: https://www.amazon.science/blog/preserving-the-privacy-of-ai-training-data
- Title: Preserving the privacy of AI training data

## Evidence

> ML systems trained on sensitive data can leak training information through membership inference, federated-learning gradient inversion, and attacks on shared FL global models; differential privacy and secure multiparty computation are presented as practical defenses.

> The post walks through three attack/defense pairs:
> - Membership inference using confidence-score thresholds learned from a proxy model; mitigated with DP-SGD.
> - Gradient inversion against federated learning local updates; mitigated with private federated learning via secure multiparty computation / secret sharing.
> - Recovery from shared FL global models; mitigated again with differential privacy.

> The authors report 97% precision among records flagged as training data in a ResNet-50/ImageNet-1k membership inference attack; DP-SGD on EMNIST reached 78% test accuracy at ε=1.5 and 82% at ε=3.0 versus 90% without DP; their gradient inversion attack recovered single-sample batches exactly and 3 samples from a batch of 7; under their PFL protocol, the attack could not reconstruct any training samples.

> The post notes that smaller, domain-specific models on sensitive datasets are especially vulnerable, while DP introduces an accuracy/privacy tradeoff; it also says empirical understanding of DP’s effective guarantees against attacks continues to evolve. The excerpted item text appears truncated before the final FL-global-model mitigation details are fully elaborated.
